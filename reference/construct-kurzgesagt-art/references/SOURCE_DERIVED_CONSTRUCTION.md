**Research state:** iterative, source-grounded visual-art investigation; **not** a production-qualified style skill or Claude Code implementation. This document is an advancement of the earlier per-sequence repair report, not a replacement for the original evidence. It describes concrete illustrated forms and how to construct them, *before* downstream prompt engineering.

**Source actually available:** fifteen selected intervals from five films, totaling **58.2 seconds / 3,492 frame positions**. All positions have chronological **384 × 216 thumbnail witnesses** grouped in one-second contact pages; selected positions have 1920 × 1080 native witnesses. They are not five complete MP4s and cannot establish native-resolution study of all ~67 minutes. The frame-ledger filenames below refer to the locally materialized evidence packet. Statements about fine artwork use full-HD witnesses; statements about motion are checked against the sequential contacts. We have not created an unfamiliar output or tested a production model's adherence.

## The operating question, applied inside every artistic cluster

For each actual source subject, investigate the **complete visible drawing and its changing states**, then ask: **What important artistic choice would an illustrator still have to make up if given only this explanation?** Convert each such missing choice into a source-answerable group of specific questions, return to neighboring frames or comparable source subjects, and rewrite the construction procedure. Repeat *before* declaring the observation good enough for a style skill. Never replace the visible answer with a theory about what an artist “wanted to evoke.”

“Molecular” here means **fully resolving the whole high-impact construction**: complete silhouette, subforms, drawing-state variants, color regions and how they attach, visible projection, foreground/background material, occlusions, and interactions. It does not mean measuring tiny eye gaps, inventing ratios or endlessly describing single pixels. A complete clustered method connects these components so that no large aesthetic choice disappears between them.

## A. Whole-frame visual grammar — what recurs without erasing genuine variation

### A1. Complete silhouette and attachment grammar

Begin by drawing each prominent thing as a recognizable complete *subject-specific outer form*, then construct its major parts **inside or around that form according to their actual attachments and depth**. This is observed across the human actor, expressive birds, cattle, macro fly, city buildings, vintage computer, galaxy bodies and underwater mini-world.

- A human is **not** a sphere plus identical tubular arms. Hair occupies a substantial bounded skull form, the face an integrated asymmetrical patch, clothing follows a full pose-specific torso/leg silhouette, hands connect to purposeful contact.
- A cartoon bird is **not** a generic circular icon: it has its own contiguous body/head design, beak surface, large pupil/sclera or deliberately closed-eye state, wing as an attached plate, and convincing simplified feet/legs.
- A quadruped is constructed from a whole torso/head/neck mass, proper separate near/far legs, horn/ear/nostril and occluded body markings.
- A machine is a series of connected **functional visible surfaces** (top, side, face, recess, supports, controls) rather than a decorated rectangle.
- An environment is built as a **world with material layers and perspective/contact**, not a separate backdrop sticker behind the focal subject.

**When it applies:** all meaningful subjects. **What is not universal:** rounded corners, specific character proportions, eye type, anatomy, orange/teal hues, geometry family, or any one source character.

### A2. What “flat” actually means in these sources

Many opaque illustrations use large continuous fills bounded by intentional curved or angular edges, and **secondary colored masses that describe form**. Subregions are not arbitrary decorative stripes: a face's orange cheek belongs to its peach face; the head's dark hair patch stays in its hair; a shirt's cyan inner sleeve follows the bent sleeve; a building's blue recessed window follows its wall plane; a fly's orange specular patch follows the near red eye lens.

The source does **not** enforce universally dark exterior strokes. Often the contour is *the collision of two colored masses*, with darker or lighter strips used only when the object requires a rim, recess, or bevel. Some contained secondary art is deliberately **thin linework** (desk montage pencil drawing), so a global “no strokes anywhere” rule would also be false.

Build each large surface from: **main material body → attached plane/form color patch → deliberately chosen recessed/raised feature → local edge or specular mark where visibly present**. Preserve coverage and occlusion. If a decorative mark cannot be assigned to a real illustrated subform or material, do not use it to simulate craftsmanship.

### A3. Color relationships, not an arbitrary universal palette

The source uses several dramatic conditional color regimes:

- **Warm sparse acting:** pale yellow, orange land/tree forms, saturated cyan clothing, dark navy pants/hair, violet bench and bird, small pink grounds. Clean very light background keeps action uncluttered.
- **Warm interior:** yellow-to-orange walls/graphic frames, cyan/teal furniture, magenta clothing, deep dark lower body, and brilliant cyan/magenta bird accents.
- **Clinic:** blue/cyan room planes with similar-hue low-contrast fixtures, large purple foreground hair, orange complex faces, purple chair and green clinician; the dark magenta thought form is a separate high-contrast focal element.
- **Organic farm:** green depth bands from pale distance to very dark foreground, pale peach cows, muted straw/cyan/green figures, tiny blue flies and red marks; detailed but **layered** rather than all equally vivid.
- **Macro biology:** near-black indigo and electric cobalt anatomical masses, oversized red eyes with local flat coral glints, turquoise mouth/wing; diffuse plum background.
- **Urban fabricated world:** adjoining yellow/peach/orange, cyan/blue/navy, lavender/pink/magenta families with face-specific side values and ordered built details. No generic random swatch per window.
- **Cosmic:** nearly black violet spacious field; coherent bright warm core and colored structural arcs, localized bloom, sparse stars and little independent symbol badges.

**Transfer:** for an unfamiliar subject, choose a palette role from the *visual situation and material*, then give each body its own consistent fill, shadow/plane variant, local accent and depth context. A repeated colorful palette alone does not make the style; the colors must attach to constructed forms and maintain hierarchy across the whole image.

### A4. Important conditional lighting distinction

Four different visible treatments must never collapse into “add glowing gradients”:

1. **Opaque surface shaping:** hard-edged local colored patches and face-dependent values; visible on cheeks, clothes, machines, walls, animal bodies, fly plates.
2. **Optical/specular surfaces:** bounded lens-reflection and glass shapes, sometimes near-white; visible on fly compound eyes, glasses and screens. The reflection is *inside the actual glass form*.
3. **Illuminated material:** a whole attached branch, lamp, window, ribbon or activated machine part becomes differently colored; light source geometry persists underneath the halo.
4. **Atmosphere and bloom:** softened glows around galaxy cores, neural highlights, sun/moon, brilliant point sources and whiteouts. Only the relevant areas blur; opaque anatomy and architecture often remain crisp.

This four-way distinction is one of the strongest cross-film anti-overfitting results. It determines the method, not just the adjective.

**Do not invent** realistic finger articulation, identical hand mittens for every task, rigid symmetric limb thickness or full 3D anatomy. Source uses functional stylization with highly differentiated real actions.

## C. Constructing complete worlds, spaces, architecture and functional equipment

### C1. Oblique city: geometry and architectural diversity must both be authored

Source: AI_FINAL__isometric_city 762–765.25s; particularly native_f045840 and native_f045900.

The source does not show “little isometric buildings.” It shows an *entire tightly packed developed city* in which the perspective/projection family, design of each building, facades, roofs, roads, vegetation and occlusion fit together.

**Whole spatial construction procedure:**

1. Establish one **elevated oblique view** for the complete world. The broad roof surfaces are exposed and wall edges remain coherent within each building; comparable depth-edge orientations remain repeated through the urban block. Preserve different visible front/side faces and their roof junctions. The city art should not look like unrelated clip-art facades facing arbitrary cameras.
2. Create an actual **development plan** beneath the vertical objects: small irregular corridors of lavender roads, short dashed light lane marks, green courtyards, tiny connected planted gaps, then place buildings on adjoining lots. Roads continue behind occlusion; vegetation populates plausible leftover strips. Do not make a checkerboard of colored boxes with no functioning streets or parcels.
3. Construct several distinct **building whole-form families** *before* making repetitive details:
   - **Tall ochre/peach aperture towers:** blocky vertical masses; bright cream/yellow edge columns, darker orange/pink recessed bands, many narrow red or violet vertical window openings repeated along *each actual supporting wall*. Top floors/teal roof ledges are distinct color structures, not a fill rectangle.
   - **Blue/peach bay towers:** vertically layered blue wall plus peach opposing wall; cyan/blue projecting window bays or ledge-like frames, dark navy recessed windows *inside* the bays; the bay projects and creates a real front/side relationship, not an outlined window sticker.
   - **Magenta glazed tower:** pink/violet mullion grid on one visible face, dark navy/cyan cell grid on another; supporting thin horizontal and vertical bars form an ordered plane-aligned lattice. The division of sides is deliberate.
   - **Short stepped peach dwellings:** several orange-pink wall masses, roof troughs with vivid cyan perimeter and darker inset center; windows are small warm or dark rectangles nested in the visible facade, placed differently on different walls.
   - **Lavender banded low blocks:** repeated horizontal blue-purple siding, broad cream roof slab, locally raised planter islands of dark/bright green; facade walls have low feature density.
   - **Compact bright-yellow roof blocks:** distinct flat square yellow cap with small central raised fitting and narrow edge lines, broad colored side bands. Their restraint is as designed as a dense glazed tower.
   - **Civic pitched-roof building:** tall narrow cream/peach wall, reddish orange wedge roof, violet arched window/door shapes and a connected red steeple. Pitched gables follow the same city projection without being converted to a square roof.
4. Assign each building an **internally coherent color family** (warm orange/red, blue/cyan/navy, lavender/teal etc.), then apply distinct **face-specific lighter/darker regions** that follow the drawn mass. Windows and raised details inherit that building family rather than using arbitrary rainbow bits.
5. Integrate **fine purposeful neighborhood features**: raised roof utilities, shrubs as clustered green blobby crowns, small garden beds, a church green plot with minimal short side ramps, courtyards, narrow visible street segments. These occupy correct planes and spaces.
6. Paint **strict near/far occlusion**: tall near blocks obscure distant streets, courtyard strips show between masses, near roof edges cover farther facades, and small greenery partially covers architectural bases. The scene remains richly packed without black separator strokes.

**Critical conditional evidence:** At 45840 and 45900 the same architectural families preserve their shapes as the camera travels, revealing more lower portions; the buildings are not morphing. If an unfamiliar brief needs a city, use this *complete urban grammar* and develop appropriate original building types. If the brief needs a single industrial warehouse, the next cluster overrides the city archetypes.

**What is not yet established:** alternate camera views of the same city, suburban house construction outside this scene, true depth rotation and the design method for every architectural style. One rich city shot establishes a lot about illustrated projection and detail, but not every world type.

### D1. Biological network: structured dense complexity, not colored noise

Source: MEMORIES__dense_synapses 91–95s; native_f005520, f005580, f005640 and chronological pages.

The source fills nearly the entire frame with an **organized vertical forest** of violet branching filaments. Many narrow stems run up/down and fork or join in smooth curves. Their termini have deliberately different **drawn families**, not generic evenly spaced stars:

- Upper population: tiny orange-gold pendant/drop tips, often with tiny teal/green partner lobes.
- Mid-high population: flat blue/turquoise slanted leaf-like or two-lobed pieces on dark branches, sometimes with different cyan highlights.
- Middle band: a noisy-looking but actually repeated family of orange/pink capped mushroom-like terminals, magenta pairs, round yellow centers and short warm marks.
- Lower zones: increasingly cyan-violet/blue populations mixed with magenta, fading darker near the bottom.

The stem/terminal relationships matter. Many terminals are visibly **attached to actual curved branches**, which continue behind neighboring branches and other attached tips. Their density is high but they are not detached confetti.

**Make an unfamiliar analogous network by:**

1. Define a meaningful branching connector anatomy with one principal elongated trunk segment, several forked subbranches and a small set of genuine different terminal silhouettes.
2. Build many overlapping connected branch trees, varied in bend, height and overlap; preserve enough purple negative space between trunks that the network reads as a designed *web* rather than a solid paint mass.
3. Assign coherent terminal families to broad **vertical/material bands** and local clusters, not random color over the whole page. Neighboring terminals may vary in attached geometry but remain recognized as the same biological family.
4. On top of the dark baseline network, **select whole connected branch groups** for luminous state: recolor stems warm orange, endpoints yellow/cream and place only a local soft halo around these connected structures. Keep the rest of the network visibly dark and connected; the bright area should not be a pasted spotlight circle on unrelated confetti.
5. Keep any information card/label a separate screen-space plate over the world, not part of biological anatomy.

**Observed movement:** successive full-HD images have modest displacement of background network and major changes in which gold connected clusters are illuminated. The earlier approximate registration demonstrated that illumination changes cannot be explained by the displacement alone. This permits an independently animated *lighting/activation group system* over a persistent designed network. It does **not** prove a precise one-way electrical pulse or an exact sequence of branch activation; that remains a missing source-answerable motion question.

## H. The actual iterative observation behavior — preserved as a working method

The loop is not a checklist. It is a **sequence of specific reasoning moves** inside the chosen high-impact artistic cluster:

1. **Read the whole visible image/state** and establish subject, viewpoint, complete forms, materials, environment and what is moving/changing. Determine the dominant things that would destroy the result if drawn wrong: actor silhouette, building construction, world structure, face, distinct lighting material, etc. An image with many features does not mean every tiny decoration is equally important.
2. **Start the real drawing method immediately.** For the current subject, state the assembly in order: major silhouette → connected parts and viewing faces → local color plane/shape treatment → material/functional detail → occlusion/environment → altered drawing states. Do not begin with subjective “friendliness,” “emotional cohesion,” “visual language feels dynamic” or unsupported why-statements.
3. **Notice precisely where the drawing method becomes vague.** If it says “detailed windows,” ask what *kind* of windows, on which wall/roof family, what is recessed and what projects, how near and far faces differ, how the whole building meets street and adjacent blocks. If it says “expressive person,” ask what actual brows, eye/mouth silhouettes, cheek/hair patches, body/garment forms, hand contact and alternative posture drawings are visible.
4. **Bundle those connected unknowns** to get meaningful evidence per revisit. The window question includes roof, facade, projection and neighbor occlusion; the human question includes face, hands, cloth and body relation. Visit native frames for material/shape facts and adjacent timeline pages for state transitions. Never let the search become a tiny pixel-metric rabbit hole.
5. **Change the source-derived drawing instructions based on answers.** Do not merely append the evidence quote. If the building has recessed dark windows, the method must now build recessed dark windows; if the ribbon changes underside and front overlap, the method must now manufacture correct two-faced coils.
6. **Compare a meaningfully similar *and meaningfully different* source.** City vs industrial shed; runner vs clinic vs reclining woman; blue bird vs purple bird; red eye versus galaxy glow; dense farm vs sparse yellow field. The comparison should locate which construction rule is a durable shared pattern and which remains conditional, eliminating overfit.
7. **Ask “what is missing” again on the *whole connected construction*.** Look for ambiguity in the silhouette, attached components, material, viewpoint, environment, state timing, and whole-image integration. If it is high impact and source-answerable, return and revise. If it is not supported by this source packet, preserve a precisely named gap and its needed kind of witness; don't fabricate mastery.
8. **Preserve every meaningful revision** in a short evidence-and-decision ledger, with source filenames, the previous insufficient method and the corrected operational method. Each later revisit reads the current surviving method and known exceptions, not all 3492 source frames and all earlier discussions again.

**Why this is different from formal “ten iterations”:** a new iteration is only valuable when it obtains *new source facts*, resolves a previously unsupported decision, **modifies the construction method**, or identifies a genuinely different necessary source type. Rephrasing “colorful, clean, dynamic” ten times is a single shallow pass disguised as ten.

### Concrete example of the dynamic question engine working

Start from the AI city 45840: one is tempted to report “colorful isometric buildings.” The missing draw decision is visible immediately: *how would a new building obtain its roof, facade and opening family without a model improvising colored cuboids?* The evidence shows at least seven whole distinct archetypes described in C1, so “just make cubes” is replaced by complete roof/facade/window assemblies and common oblique world projection. **Next** the method still leaves “does every architecture follow this?” vague. The factory 23100 is an explicit counterexample: shallow curved roofs, sparse door/window rhythm, huge horizontal spans and different long-road perspective. The conditional language becomes “same complete plane/part discipline, but distinct architecture families.” **Next** the factory changes to sunset and night: do we just apply a tint? Native 23160/23220 shows the geometry survives while cyan roof, wall material, gold windows, lamps, cars and sky change categorically. The same architectural cluster now contains both **structural depiction** and **alternate material-state illustration**, connected to a real environment rather than a list of detached facts.

No philosophical question was needed. Every next question came from an actual production choice that the current visual observation had not resolved.
