## The actual learning sequence exercised here

1. **Look at the complete moving image.** What are the full subjects, material classes, world and artwork states at this point? Which surfaces contain detail and which intentionally do not?
2. **Separate distinct visible constructions** before interpreting them: drawn silhouette, bounded color subregions, projection/depth, compositing, separately drawn poses, illumination, graphic overlays, camera displacement. Do not call a camera pan a shape morph; do not call a lighting patch traveling without tracking a corresponding landmark.
3. **Ask a connected bundle of high-impact questions**, not a canned checklist: *What are this subject's whole visible forms? What subforms attach to which form? How are its front, side, top, internal, shadow and active parts depicted? How do equivalent objects differ? What states have to be drawn when the scene changes?* Search for answers in adjacent frames and other family members, prioritizing the construction that determines the complete look.
4. **Write the conditional drawing procedure immediately**, preserving the evidence and its scope. This is source-derived construction knowledge, not yet a copy-paste prompt and not speculation about what the director thought.
5. **Ask “What is missing?” again in that same connected cluster.** Locate the specific unsupported high-impact choice; search further witnesses. If a new witness contradicts an earlier claim, update the method. Do not count iterations to make it appear complete.
