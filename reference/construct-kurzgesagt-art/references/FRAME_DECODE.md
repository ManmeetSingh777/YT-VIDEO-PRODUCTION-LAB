# The fifteen selected moving-source investigations — integrated visual decoding

**Read with `02_CONDITIONAL_ILLUSTRATION_LANGUAGE.md`.** This document describes the actual illustrated construction, transitions and temporal behavior in the fifteen recovered source intervals. It is *not* fifteen independent aesthetic descriptions. Each cluster asks connected, directly answerable questions about the source's **visible illustrated objects, their construction, and changing states**, then resolves them into transferable artistic knowledge and contrasts. ## Integrated discovery across all fifteen moving intervals

Across the five films, **the constant is not one palette, one character template, one density level, or one universal motion routine**. The consistent *observed construction language* is: (1) complete designed subject silhouettes appropriate to what the subject is; (2) deliberate large color fields and attached material/form-specific interior regions; (3) spatially coherent overlap and grounding; (4) organized rich detail where it serves the subject and strong simplicity elsewhere; (5) different illustration treatments for different materials and viewpoints while keeping a common controlled clean-finish discipline; (6) actual alternate drawing and material states for changes that cannot be reproduced by sliding one bitmap; and (7) coherent compositing of these illustrated layers during camera, world, light, object and transition animation.

### The distinctions that emerge *only* through cross-reference

- The computer and city use layered planar facet faces and functional facade marks; the fly uses curved lens facets and biological plates; the neural field uses branching networks; the galaxy uses ring/spiral luminance. They all contain detailed internal structure, but that structure is **material-specific**, not one universal pattern.
- The purple bird is as intentionally designed as the microscopic fly even though it uses far fewer shapes. Complexity is assigned by need and scale, not a generic art-generation richness parameter.
- Near-character cinematic movement requires changing expression, limbs and contact; a huge city with stable geometry can use camera progression; a color-cycle factory requires semantic material states; a morphing thought object requires completely new contours and contents; a galaxy flare requires purpose-built light-ray art. These are distinct animation grammars.
- Dark scenes include many different darkness constructions: a dark cosmic void and luminous spiral bodies, a violet farm under moonlight with localized yellow windows, a purple thought-object inside a cyan clinic, and a navy insect against magenta. A single 'dark neon Kurzgesagt' prompt is not adequate.
- Foreground humans in POV, runners, reclining figures, workers and clinicians differ in head view, hair silhouette, garments and required hands. The artist language is transferable only if it supports these divergent specific constructions.

The 15 selections provide visual evidence for those conditional artistic methods.
