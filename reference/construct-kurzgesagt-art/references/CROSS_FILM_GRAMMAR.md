# Source-derived conditional illustration language

**What this document is:** concrete drawing and animated-construction language grounded in the selected 15 moving-source windows. It is organized by *how visual things are actually made*, not by supposed private intentions of the artists. It is the intended knowledge source for a future style-controlling skill. Do **not** turn any one source subject, subject color, composition or character into a universal template. References in square brackets identify the recovered source intervals; corresponding original frames and native witnesses are stored in `witnesses/`.

## A. Whole-image visual identity: source-grounded rules

### A1 — Make an entire illustrated object read as one authored silhouette before adding parts
**Source:** running person [BRAIN_BLINDS__human_acting], seated human and bird [BRAIN_BLINDS__red_object], fly [NUCLEAR_FLIES__fly_macro], cow and farmer [NUCLEAR_FLIES__bio_world], two bird mascots [SOLAR_SYSTEM__mascots_interaction].

**Visible construction:** Each subject has a coherent large outer contour that distinguishes its species/identity/action. The human's hair is one shaped black-navy mass, with small constructed tufts rather than dozens of hair strands. Clothing forms a complete torso/arms/legs silhouette; its colored fold and cuff pieces belong to those garment masses. The bird mascot is a single pear/teardrop-like dark body with a visually distinct wing mass, eye, beak and feet. The cow has a long complete torso, neck/head, udders/patches, separate near/far legs, a tapered tail and an attached ground shadow. The fly has a head/thorax/abdomen mass, separate eye and antenna profiles, legs and translucent wing shapes. None can be built merely by attaching uniform cylinders to a head circle. The silhouette construction varies decisively by subject.

**Conditional construction method:** First build the *whole* original subject with its primary mass/silhouette and chosen facing, view, pose and action. Form internal eyes, beaks, muscles, clothing, spots, facets and attachments as appropriately designed *subregions* or linked pieces. Demand the visually correct silhouette across required poses, not identity-preserving point transforms alone. Different art families need different internal anatomy (human facial planes, insect compound lens, bird beak/wing, cow muzzle/legs).

**Never generalize:** constant head-body ratio, circular eyes for every creature, universal mitten hands, tube limbs, same pose, same density or same exact contour curvature.

### A2 — Use contiguous colored regions to model the form; use drawn marks only where the source genuinely uses them
**Source:** computer housing [AI_FINAL__machine_interface], fly head and lens [NUCLEAR_FLIES__fly_macro], bird and cloth [BRAIN_BLINDS__red_object], animal world [NUCLEAR_FLIES__bio_world], isometric architecture [AI_FINAL__isometric_city].

**Visible construction:** Large areas are bold, clean, contiguous fills. Internal art is built from additional colored shapes: the fly's blue and teal head marks are bounded patches that follow head placement; its red lens has a field of darker red interlocking facet cells, with several differently colored/specular oval marks. The computer has separate lavender casing surfaces, darker screen inset, shadowed recess, vent strips and aligned buttons. The runner's sleeves have light blue and darker cyan regions while the torso remains recognizably one garment. The buildings have distinct top/side/front planes and inset facade bands. A black outline around every object is **not** a reliable cross-film rule.

**Conditional construction method:** Construct form color with a hierarchy of large local fill, secondary form-following region, small subject-relevant mark and limited highlight/shadow. Position each patch on the correct material and surface. Preserve legibility of large shape beneath details. Use an explicit stroke only when the particular illustrated object uses it; do not apply generic sticker outlines or coarse stroke to all regions.

### A3 — Detail is *designed content* attached to subject anatomy and material, not texture noise
**Source:** fly macro, neuron field, city facade, farm, comic drawing inset, galaxy.

**Visible construction:** Each rich material carries a different kind of detail. Insect eye: nested red facet geometry and compact specular highlights. Architecture: aligned window rows, roof protrusions, balconies and recesses. Fly body: blue spots and elongated plates attached to head/thorax volumes. Biological network: connected stems and branching terminal forms with colored tips. Food inset: individually irregular edible pieces within a bowl. Space: stars, orbital spiral bands and glow layers attached to celestial forms. The illustrated detail isn't spread uniformly across the canvas.

**Conditional construction:** Determine what the subject *is made of* and what visible structural/material signatures that subject needs. Add characteristic internal forms that follow its contour/plane/depth. Keep distant counterparts simpler where actually present. Avoid random speckles, unrelated glyphs or repeating one decoration across different materials.

### A4 — Whole-image coherence tolerates major differences in subject complexity
**Source:** visually simple bird atop detailed star field [SOLAR_SYSTEM__mascots_interaction]; actor versus mechanical computer [AI_FINAL__machine_interface]; detailed fly versus tiny farm creatures [NUCLEAR_FLIES__fly_macro; __bio_world]; woman and detailed changing object versus simplified clinic furniture [MEMORIES__clinic_interaction].

**Visible construction:** Art density is not uniform. A solid, instantly readable foreground mascot and a complex star cluster coexist. The operator is simplified beside a functional, multi-plane computer. The clinic retains sparse drawers and wall objects around the expressive faces and elaborate central thought illustration.

**Conditional method:** Keep a coherent sharp graphic illustration finish while allocating detail by subject's scale, distinctive material and compositional role. Design simplified objects as complete silhouettes with deliberate few divisions, not unfinished sketches. Design dense focal items with actual structured detail. Do not force every pixel to be detailed or every character to be equally simplified.

## B. Characters, animals, faces, and physical acting

### B1 — Human head: a coherent hair skull mass and a deliberately designed facial area
**Source:** running woman, reclining person, clinic patients/staff, retro computer operator.

**Visual method:** For the runner, the large black/dark-navy hair mass retains a highly recognizable upper contour, bun/topknot and a few tapered tufts; ear/skin shapes are part of the head. Eyebrow strokes and white/black eye forms are arranged within warm skin color. The mouth can become a large dark open interior with lighter skin/lip boundaries; facial silhouette and eye shapes visibly change with exertion. The seated character has a separate hair/ear arrangement, short eyebrow and rounded cheek. Clinic characters use large dark-violet hair, differently shaded warm face planes, glasses on one character and distinct white eyes with dark pupils. The operator is seen from behind and accordingly does not gratuitously display facial details.

**Conditional method:** Form a subject-specific complete hairstyle and facial volume with a view-dependent amount of visible face. Incorporate eye/brow/mouth as an intentionally related facial drawing; plan separate art states for strong expression or head angle changes. Glasses/headwear rest on the correct volume and occlude it properly. Do not reuse one 3/4 face sticker across unrelated identities, simply rotate a front image, or make a rear-view character face forward so its expression is visible.

### B2 — Human gestures require complete arm, hand, prop, and contact drawing states
**Source:** runner with flying small round object and bird/bench [BRAIN_BLINDS__human_acting]; hand on spoon and game controller [BRAIN_BLINDS__first_person]; operator hands above keyboard [AI_FINAL__machine_interface]; farmer with cow and second human [NUCLEAR_FLIES__bio_world]; clinician clipboard [MEMORIES__clinic_interaction].

**Visual method:** Fingers/hand shapes in these views are simplified but recognizable. The running figure's two arms do not remain symmetric: sleeves rotate/change silhouette; one hand opens as a round magenta item moves away, the other reaches toward the bird/bench. Legs have distinct bent and extended shape arrangements, feet and oval ground shadow register with the body. In first-person, arms occupy large separate foreground masses, hands wrap a spoon or controller in physically recognizable contact. The farmman's arm/hand overlap with the cow's surface. Clinician's one hand supports a tablet while other hand changes gesture.

**Conditional method:** Before animating a gesture, draw the actual hand-to-object contact state, the release/after-contact state, the near/far arm overlap, adjacent sleeve shape, support/contact surface and shadows. Require full illustrated poses at consequential action phases. The path of the object alone is not an adequate manufacturing procedure; neither is an IK skeleton with one permanent hand drawing.

### B3 — Simplified animal mascots are not universally simplified biological subjects
**Source:** purple bird/green glasses bird [SOLAR_SYSTEM__mascots_interaction]; couch bird [BRAIN_BLINDS__red_object]; macro fly [NUCLEAR_FLIES__fly_macro]; cattle [NUCLEAR_FLIES__bio_world].

**Visible differences:** Small blue and purple birds are readable with compact bulbous torsos, wing masses, eyes, simple hooked beaks, thin legs and clear feet. The insect body is larger, heavily articulated visually, with eye/lens faceting, cyan mouth, protrusions, multiple segmented legs and decorated/translucent wings. Cows use a distinct quadruped torso-muzzle-udder-horn/ear-leg grammar with integrated spots. These are *different conditional productions*, not a single rounded-forms style.

**Conditional method:** Select species- and purpose-appropriate design complexity: iconic mask-like mascots for small illustrative companions, much richer shape/segment treatment for magnified biological instruction, whole quadruped proportions for distant farm animals. Preserve the cross-film clean filled graphic language but do not transfer fly facets onto birds or bird proportions onto humans.

### B4 — Animal/insect anatomy receives material-local colored structures
**Source:** fly 313–315.95. 

**Visual construction:** Build the fly from a dark indigo/narrowly faceted central head with asymmetric sapphire/blue paneling, protruding tapered blue rods or hair-like pieces, round scarlet eye lobes composed of irregular polygonal darker facets, bright cream oval highlights and small warm yellow/orange eye dots. Large purple-blue legs fold at distinct angles and have rounded/tapered changes in width; pale cyan wings have banding and light spots, framed in darker cyan boundary edges. The mouth/snout is turquoise/mint and includes a distinct dark overlay, with small lighter protrusions below. Different colored parts hold separate relationships when the whole figure drifts.

**Conditional method:** For a *different* magnified insect, identify its actual body-plan, appendage arrangement, lens/compound eye presence, transparent wing material and mouthparts. Design each with the corresponding observed *art-treatment family* rather than copying the fly's shape. Treat shininess, facets and translucency as structured colored drawings, not realistic photo material. Distinct views require proper redesigned occlusion and edge positions, not blind rotation of one full insect drawing.

## C. Environments, worlds, and architecture

### C1 — Organic landscape is assembled from differently illustrated depth bands
**Source:** farm [NUCLEAR_FLIES__bio_world], desert path/hills [BRAIN_BLINDS__human_acting], cosmic forest camp [SOLAR_SYSTEM__technology_action], first-person fields [BRAIN_BLINDS__first_person].

**Visual construction:** The farm sky uses a light cyan-green field with bounded soft cloud clusters and far dark-bird strokes; the far hills are rolling olive/green masses, with small tree crowns on different scales. A fence creates a clear midground plane. The foreground uses overlapping dark green shrubs, individually shaped grass tufts and small yellow blooms. Objects touch ground with separate dark oval patches and leg positions. The desert has large intentionally open pale yellow areas, a winding lighter path, orange silhouettes of small trees and distant architectural silhouettes; the darker/stronger actor remains legible. The first-person field uses a low clear horizon and many stylized green blade clusters; foreground arms occupy prominent screen space.

**Conditional method:** Construct complete environments from intentional back/mid/foreground illustrated layers; specify recognizable species/landscape-specific silhouettes and materials, not generic repeated curves. Scale/color/detail distribution must follow the scene's depth and spatial layout. Reserve some coherent negative space for subject movement. Place cast shadows and occlusion where objects actually ground, rather than float.

### C2 — Isometric city uses consistent perspective with genuinely differentiated buildings
**Source:** AI_FINAL city 762–765.25. 

**Visual construction:** Most structures have visible front, side and roof/top treatments; roof faces are bright distinct polygons with inner ledges, rims, planters or inset roof wells. Tall narrow towers have vertical window columns, some with long colored spandrel bands, some with smaller window grids. Lower homes have stepped parapets and alternating colored facades; one small church-like building has a pitched roof and steeple that respects the same illustrated angled worldview. Bright teal, orange, violet, lilac, pink and blue form adjacent large coherent surface planes, not a spray of highlights. Green treetops/courtyards fill spaces between blocks. Apparent city movement largely preserves architectural geometry within its scene.

**Conditional method:** Decide on one coherent oblique projection for the scene, then draw each building as a complete distinctive structure with a functional roof silhouette, inset and facade system. Vary footprints, heights, zoning and rooftop identity while maintaining edge/perspective coherence. Add window and trim distributions following the facade planes and architectural role. Do not clone the same cuboid a hundred times or add randomly spaced windows. The specific city palette and church are subject-dependent, not mandatory style ingredients.

### C3 — Functional machinery uses nested physical planes and believable attachment
**Source:** retro computer [AI_FINAL__machine_interface], technology triptych [SOLAR_SYSTEM__technology_action], floating clinic thought object [MEMORIES__clinic_interaction].

**Visual construction:** The computer is a complete purple box form with a brighter top edge, side vents following the facing plane, a thick bezel and dark recessed glass screen, grouped buttons on left and a physically separate sloping keyboard. Both components cast coherent shaped dark shadows. In the tech triptych, the rocket has elongated cylindrical/tapered body, fins and distinct engine glow/launch cloud; the central machine has stacked cylindrical platforms and vertical cyan-lit channels; the right blue toroidal machine shows concentric layered rings, numbered/cut panels and a central orange bright orbital element. These are distinct mechanical object types with consistent meaningful attachment and silhouette.

**Conditional method:** Break an unfamiliar machine into functional physical subassemblies: primary housing, panels, apertures, mechanisms, material-specific markings, supports, recesses and visual emissions. Draw aligned plane colors and physically grounded overlaps. Assign bright accent only to actual active/emissive parts. Do not request generic sci-fi boxes, randomly scattered lines or equally glowing trim everywhere.

### C4 — Small enclosed worlds can be complex without losing silhouette hierarchy
**Source:** miniature ocean/fish world [MEMORIES__portrait].

**Visual construction:** The world is a rounded cyan/blue bowl or sectioned sphere with a heavy violet rim, lighter inner water surface and a decorated underside with small purplish aquatic markings. Numerous different coral branches, leafy plants, bright fish shapes and a little diver/actor sit within the bounded body of water. The backdrop is deep purple near-black, and a bright cyan atmospheric halo surrounds the inner water. Large white forms enter the upper composition later, changing the apparent enclosing viewpoint. Tiny fish have clear head/tail/fin silhouettes instead of arbitrary colored confetti.

**Conditional method:** For an original small isolated world, start with an unambiguous enclosing/container silhouette and perspective. Construct a complete local environment inside it, with layered miniature species or objects whose material and scale are coherent; integrate rim, inner surface and volume shading. Use the surrounding dark void to preserve contour and glow as appropriate. This is *not* a universal instruction to put every scientific scene inside a bowl.

## D. Color, lighting, shadows and surface effects

### D1 — Color is relational and scene-conditional, not a locked five-color swatch
**Source:** orange desert vs sunny farm vs purple night industrial campus vs underwater cyan clinic vs city vs purple cosmic field. 

**Visual evidence:** The farm uses muted grass greens, warm cow cream and brown, bright accent hats/skin; the desert uses pale yellow/ochre with navy and magenta; the clinic uses blue/cyan room values with purple hair and warm orange faces; the city uses adjacent intense teal, orange, lilac, magenta and navy planes; dark space uses near-black navy fields with magenta/cyan/violet glows; the factory transitions through pink sunset to deep violet/blue night while keeping cyan roofs. Such variation is too large for one fixed palette.

**Conditional method:** Select palette by depicted world, light, focal subjects and material role. Preserve purposeful local groupings, distinguish foreground/background values, make each material internally coherent, and reserve high-saturation small accents for relevant visual functions. Explicitly prohibit copying one observed color layout onto unrelated environments. Specific hex measurements can support a verified local asset, but there is no justification for globalizing one sampled RGB code.

### D2 — Most everyday surfaces use illustrated color patches, while luminous subjects may add soft glow
**Source:** computer and actor, fly, city, sky, galaxies, day-night campus, erupting warm objects.

**Visual construction:** Human faces often have warm darker cheek/ear or brow regions and some clean hard boundaries. The fly's body is dark with bounded blue shape patches; its eyes are flat red graphic facets plus separate crisp glints. Machine casing side/front regions are flat but value-shifted. The cosmic galaxy core uses soft white-cyan and magenta illumination transitioning into distinct spiral band structures, with scattered bright stars. Night factory shows discrete yellow lit windows, warm pools below poles and moon halo; not every object gets a generic rim light.

**Conditional method:** Classify each surface as ordinary material, glossy/specular, translucent, emissive or distant atmospheric. Ordinary illustrated material: shape-specific fill patches and restrained localized highlights. Emissive subject: bright core/shape, well-defined bright inner portions and conditional soft falloff into adjacent materials. Specular eye/lens: few crisp well-placed highlight forms, plus structured colored interior. Avoid applying bloom and color gradients indiscriminately, and do not ban soft gradients where actually observed.

### D3 — Time-of-day changes are new illustrated material states, not merely a global darkening
**Source:** factory, [NUCLEAR_FLIES__landscape] 384–388.

**Visible construction:** Underlying long factory building shells, stepped curved roofs, road and tiny vehicles remain recognizable through daylight, sunset and night. Sky changes from light purple/pink into warm orange sunset and then deep navy. Facades shift among brown/orange and purple/dark blue values, roofs remain distinct cyan/turquoise, foreground fades into deep navy, little windows and lamps appear as warm yellow local-emissive spots, and a moon becomes visible. Several changes overlap and occur on different time schedules, rather than all pixels becoming darker at once.

**Conditional method:** For day/night or environmental lighting, manufacture/define the alternate color regions and visible light objects for each major world component. Preserve geometry where the same world persists; animate scene-specific changes to sky, building planes, windows, road, vegetation, shadows and lights. Keep unaffected materials coherent across states. Only use a global overlay as a supplemental effect if its artistic result is consistent with the reference; it is not sufficient alone.

### D4 — Cosmic luminosity has a structural illustrated body
**Source:** [SOLAR_SYSTEM__cosmic_world] 15–19.

**Visual construction:** A large cyan/cobalt elliptical galaxy has a brilliant white/pale core, uneven ring bands shaded violet/blue, spiral arm marks, scattered small white/cyan stars and compact sparkles. Separate orange-peach spirals have their own golden cores and orange rings; small distant galaxies receive reduced graphic structure. The backdrop is very dark purple/navy, with nonuniform star sizes/brightness. A map-like circular pin/ring accent appears near the large main spiral. A radial streak/white bloom later temporarily fills the composition and gives way to new galaxy marks.

**Conditional method:** Compose one or more complete luminous bodies with layered core, major spiral/outer band, glow, star/particle families and the correct scale differences between foreground and distant bodies. Use several nested structurally meaningful shapes and controlled atmosphere; do not approximate the galaxy with one blurred circle plus uniform sparkles. Apply bright white only to source-like luminous cores and selective glints; the source doesn't demand that all scientific illustrations glow.

## E. Animation, camera, occlusion, changing drawings

### E1 — Different kinds of motion need different artistic manufacture
**Source:** runner, scene montage, camera move over city, living cow farm, changing thought object, cyclic factory lighting, technology panels.

**Visual evidence:** The runner's limbs and facial features visibly change illustration state while the prop departs and the background holds roughly steady. The machine operator's figure is largely stable while the screen text appears and a different close composition is presented. The city is primarily a scene camera/composition drift over detailed architecture. The clinic's dark floating center evolves into a radially spiky and internally illustrated form while room geometry persists. The factory's geometry persists while semantically grouped colors and illuminations change. A single `translate` or `rotate` operation cannot model all those cases.

**Conditional method:** Identify which visual appearance is moving: camera/world, rigid whole asset, articulated parts, alternately drawn silhouette, mutable structure, material color field, material mask, or animated effect. Manufacture necessary asset versions/layers BEFORE running the sequence; use timelines to coordinate them. Do not impose one universal animation route.

### E2 — Actor and prop motion is a connected scene illustration problem
**Source:** runner 385–389, farm 82–86, clinic 752–756, bird/ribbon 177–181.

**Visible construction:** The running person changes overall arm and leg silhouettes while the released magenta object follows its own arc and small birds are other animated participants. The cow's position, farmer's crouched action and another human's syringe hand are spatially related; feet/shoes and animal hooves attach convincingly to ground. Clinician's hand gesture changes while tablet remains a separate held object. The mascot birds have different body and eye/wing states as the broad golden ribbon flies from the right and wraps near a galaxy-like cluster.

**Conditional method:** Write the visual event in terms of actual participating illustrated subjects and required artwork states, then specify motion relationships (contact, release, pursuit, gaze, overlap, support, occlusion and independent secondary activity). In the art preparation step, request *specific pose/expression/hand states* and background clean plates and masks. Camera motion is a separate operation unless evidence shows a coupled move.

### E3 — Morphing objects preserve or reconstruct artistic internals during silhouette change
**Source:** clinic floating entity 752–756; golden ribbon 177–181; first-person framing border; volcano/forest source from separate earlier calibration.

**Visible construction:** The clinic object begins as a dark purple/black soft blob with cyan/pink accent; its contour swells, then large spike-like projections, colored interior flame/drawing and a figure appear while nearby humans remain in place. The ribbon is not one solid yellow line: it has broad yellow surfaces, warm orange underside, cream highlights, changing loop occlusion and new coils that overlap the background at distinct depths. The first-person framing border is a multi-color uneven wave/zigzag stack surrounding a rectangular inner scene, with persistent foreground head/hands.

**Conditional method:** Create a clearly specified set of illustrated shape states for an original changing object, including interior material marks and correct front/back layering. Animate its state change by controlled masks/drawing replacements and deformable geometry capable of preserving a credible whole image. A plain shape interpolation that ignores material/occlusion will not reproduce the illustrated treatment.

### E4 — Graphic inset montage uses actual complete sub-art and controlled framing
**Source:** montage [AI_FINAL__desk_pan]; technology triptych [SOLAR_SYSTEM__technology_action].

**Visible construction:** The moving activity-wall has a dark paneled backing with large framed scenes; each inset uses its own coherent subject-specific illustration: bicycle wheel/legs on yellow, a food pan with numerous food pieces on dark teal, a drawing hand and paper with a line-art figure on white, a guitar player on purple, and other cropped moments. The large background wall scroll/pan reveals new insets at screen edges. The technology transition moves through an almost white flash into three simultaneously visible slanted panels, respectively rocket, structured vertical machine and bright circular chamber. Panel boundaries are white angled separators, not arbitrary split-screen random borders.

**Conditional method:** Prepare each complete inset scene independently and then composite them into a controlled multi-panel environment with intentional masks, boundaries, scale and relative motion. Establish which inset content continues to animate; design the art inside each region with its relevant conditional language. Do not replace the whole with a collage of abstract signs or off-style stock illustrations.

### E5 — Graphic attention elements are visibly separate objects rather than decorative noise
**Source:** '10,000 Connections' cyan label in neural field, text inside retro monitor, location pin in galaxy, multiple panel separators.

**Visible construction:** The neural label is a clean bounded cyan rectangle with readable white text over a deep complicated field. The retro monitor's bright yellow 'ELIZA' appears inside the dark glass display with a recessed housing around it. The cosmic pin has its own outlined container and pointed attachment. Three technology fields are legibly divided by white sloping bands.

**Conditional method:** Assign labels, callouts, in-world screens, highlight rings and separators their actual geometric ownership and depth. Draw them to remain readable against the complete artwork. Do not bake critical editable type into an uncontrolled image when motion or precise wording matters. Directional reasons for choosing specific text/labels are outside this visual grammar; their visible construction is part of it.

## F. Evidence-backed distinctions that must not be erased

- **Not all backgrounds should be densely detailed.** Farm supports a populated landscape; desert running scene deliberately reserves large simple ground zones; neural field is intentionally hyper-dense; clinic is intentionally comparatively sparse; space distributes small points around large luminous forms.
- **Not all characters need the same face.** Iconic birds, front/side human faces, back-facing operator, cows and fly have genuinely different construction. A 'same ratio/eye' rule would destroy the correct art.
- **Not all shadows are black.** Scene-specific darker color regions, oval grounding marks and luminous shadows differ across sources.
- **Not all highlights are blurred.** Hard colored facets and crisp eye glints coexist with softly luminous cores and sky atmosphere.
- **Not all animation is rigged.** Alternate illustrations, composited panels, mutable objects, scene tint changes, camera travel and cuts occur in the selected sequence family.
- **High-impact molecular depth means better, fuller visible instructions.** It does not mean measuring meaningless eye spacing or forcing any numerical geometry that does not control the appearance.

## G. The knowledge-to-asset handoff (later production, not part of source observation)

A later skill should own the applicable conditional methods above and select real visual evidence, not let a freeform image-model prompt invent the visual style. The source learning specifies **what visual language to apply and how**, while the original brief supplies unfamiliar content. The resulting art-manufacturing instruction should include the complete illustrated subject, required internal color and material subregions, appropriate views/poses, environment integration, layers and animation states. An image model may draw the full authored artwork; code, compositing and editing can preserve/control motion and exact graphic structure. But the execution route must be chosen for the art problem, not imposed as a universal SVG or image-generation solution.

The source-derived methods above cover major patterns *visible in these references*. They cannot uniquely recover unseen original keyframes, authoring layers or an original creator's every private decision. They also cannot prove that a particular provider will produce equivalent artwork. That last claim requires evidence from production, but **does not justify skipping deep source observation**.
