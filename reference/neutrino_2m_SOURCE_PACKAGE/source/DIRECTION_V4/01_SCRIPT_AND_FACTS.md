# Locked scientific narrative and narration spine

## Audience promise
**Question:** How can about 100 trillion invisible particles pass through your body each second, and how can scientists possibly see one?
**Viewer experience:** immediate astonishment → chase the invisible particles back to their source → discover they ignore the Earth → learn why → wonder how anyone can detect them → immense instrument lights up → return to personal cosmic wonder.
**Final knowledge:** Neutrinos are exceptionally abundant, electrically neutral particles that interact only very rarely with matter; neutrino detectors look for rare secondary signals. This rain is real, not a threat or magical force.

## Exact 120-second original narration (timing guide; adjust delivery speed not facts)

**0:00–0:11 (hook, one visual event in first frame)**
Something is passing through you right now. Not one thing. Not a thousand. Roughly a hundred trillion tiny particles, every single second. And you feel absolutely nothing. Right through your breakfast.

**0:11–0:23 (identify and begin causal journey)**
They are called neutrinos. To see where most of the ones around us come from, we have to leave this room, leave Earth, and dive straight into the Sun. 

**0:23–0:36 (source)**
Deep in its core, atomic nuclei fuse together. As the Sun makes the energy that keeps Earth alive, it also releases streams of these ghostlike messengers. 

**0:36–0:48 (travel and visual escalation)**
They race outward at almost the speed of light. Through space. Past the Moon. Into Earth's atmosphere. And some can even change from one kind of neutrino into another as they travel.

**0:48–1:01 (reveal: planet seems transparent)**
Your skin doesn't stop them. Your bones don't stop them. For most of these particles, even an entire planet is surprisingly easy to pass through. Including the ground beneath your feet.

**1:01–1:13 (scientific explanation)**
Not because atoms are just empty holes. Neutrinos have no electric charge, and they interact with ordinary matter incredibly rarely. Imagine an entire city where almost every door is invisible to you.

**1:13–1:25 (new dramatic question)**
So how do scientists study something that almost never touches anything? They build a detector so enormous that, eventually, one of those incredibly rare interactions becomes visible. 

**1:25–1:38 (instrument + measurable payoff)**
Deep in Antarctic ice, thousands of sensors wait in a cubic kilometer of crystal-clear darkness. When a sufficiently energetic neutrino interacts, the charged particles it creates can produce a sudden flash of blue light.

**1:38–1:49 (knowledge payoff)**
Scientists don't see the neutrino directly. They see the light and reconstruct what happened. Some of these messengers have travelled from violent events far beyond our solar system. 

**1:49–2:00 (circular, personal ending)**
So the universe is not just something you look at. It's moving straight through you. Most of it will leave no trace. But once in a while, it tells us a story.

## Claims that must remain correct
- ~100 trillion per second through the human body: IceCube Quick Facts: https://icecube.wisc.edu/about-us/facts/
- Neutrinos produced in solar fusion, pass through matter rarely, no electric charge, three types that can change in flight: Fermilab: https://neutrinos.fnal.gov/sources/solar-neutrinos/ ; NASA: https://science.nasa.gov/universe/glossary/
- IceCube has 5,160 digital optical sensors in ~1 cubic kilometer Antarctic ice; secondary charged particles can produce Cherenkov blue light: https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/research/2022/10/newly-discovered-optical-effect-allows-icecube-to-deduce-ice-crystal-properties/
- Some high-energy neutrinos come from extragalactic sources: https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/neutrino-blazar-FAQ/
- Not all high-energy neutrinos pass through the entire Earth, because interaction probability grows with energy; avoid showing literal 100% transparency for every energy: https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/press-releases/2017/11/first-look-at-how-the-earth-stops-high-energy-neutrinos-in-their-tracks/

## Correct visual metaphors
- Graphic neon streaks symbolize *invisible* neutrinos. Make them explicitly an explanatory visual metaphor, not literal glowing visible particles.
- Change in 'flavor' may be shown with a shape morph, but it is a scientific change of type, **not** a particle physically splitting into multiple visible creatures.
- IceCube sees light produced by **secondary charged particles**, not by a glowing neutrino itself.
- Nuclei fusing is a simplified representation; don't confuse electrons with protons, or show magnetic fields capturing neutrinos.
