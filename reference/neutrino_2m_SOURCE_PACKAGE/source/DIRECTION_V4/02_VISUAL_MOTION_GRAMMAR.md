# Visual and Motion Grammar — V4

**Reference goal:** Premium, visually sophisticated 2D science explainers: original highly finished custom vector scenes, kinetic explanatory metaphors, character appeal, bold graphic readability, energetic but legible pacing, and purposeful transitions. These are our own design rules, not a request to copy another studio's characters/illustrations or its exact scene layouts.

## A. Original illustrated world — design system, not generic icons

Create a unified editorial illustration style for this film. Working palette: midnight ink `#111C3D`, deep ultramarine `#284F95`, sea-glass `#33C8B7`, bright citrus `#F3D85F`, warm coral `#F46E67`, ice-cyan `#7CDCF5`, chalk white `#FFF0DA`. This is a STARTING palette, which the art director may improve with visible proof. Contrasting night palettes, not every color in every frame.

- Characters: one small human with distinctive **asymmetric coral hair curl**, mustard raincoat, expressive low-detail face, legible readable poses (rear, side, 3/4), original proportions. Not a bird, Kurzgesagt mascot or copyrighted character. Their umbrella unexpectedly fails to block neutrino 'rain' in the opening. Model character proportions so head, hands and torso preserve identity throughout.
- Environments: fully authored interior, curved Earth with atmosphere and visibly layered city/terrain, detailed solar core with plausible schematic fusion, Antarctic ice, cavernous cubic-kilometer instrumentation. Each needs coherent perspective, palette, object vocabulary and purposeful environmental details.
- Design at TWO scales: readable silhouette/composition at thumbnail size; delightful details when viewed at 1080p. Fine textures and easter eggs are subordinate to the core explanation.
- 2.5D depth: foreground silhouettes/occlusion, midground characters, far environment with carefully staggered velocities and atmospheric values. Use 3D only if genuinely needed, not for rendering a geometry experiment.
- Vector craft: intentional compound shapes, interesting negative space, polished gradients, rim highlights, designed shadow planes, occasional bloom/texture, masks and rich shape variation. Don't equate polish with drop shadows or endless glows.
- Art consistency: SAME character design, neutrino symbols and material language throughout. Science visual metaphors should be intuitively recognizable; neutrinos may be shown as small 3-point sea-glass sparks, symbolic not literal, and must have persistent identity across scale changes.
- Every explanatory scene needs visible *cause → change → consequence*. Pretty astronomy wallpaper is not an explanation.

## B. Animation must feel authored by animators

For each main action, specify at minimum (a) anticipation, (b) decisive action and acceleration, (c) follow-through/secondary effects, (d) settling, or state why that action is deliberately exempt. Avoid universal elastic easing: a rigid detector, organic arm, accelerating camera and tiny spark obey different motion personalities.

- **Tempo:** first moving visual surprise begins at 0:00, not a title slate. During explanatory beats, something meaningful changes in the visuals approximately every 2–5 seconds (a new idea, framing, staging, reveal or causal action). Shorter accents and longer comprehension pauses are intentional. Do NOT blindly cut every 3.5 seconds or drag one camera move across 15 seconds with no new idea.
- **Scene choreography:** hero action stays visually prominent; 4–15 supporting activities may be active while extra minor background particles/objects run on deterministic lightweight instancing. A thousand equal-weight objects is noise, not cinematic complexity. Show foreground/midground/background interaction and localized reactions.
- **Transformation standard:** objects actually morph, split, unfold, rotate with perspective cues, reveal hidden layers, or hand off a visual anchor to a new scene. Scaling an unchanged flat PNG is NOT a scale revelation. A 'hero transformation' has multiple states and visible causal preparation and payoff.
- **Camera choreography:** pick shot sizes (intimate character, diagrammatic, cutaway, wide-scale reveal). Camera movement changes viewer understanding, never merely decorates. Use stable focal anchors and real parallax for movement across illustrated layers, avoid arbitrary camera shake, jitter, random zoom.
- **Character acting:** responsive eyes, head lead, torso follow, arms, readable weight, compression, micro-pauses, reaction poses, emotion with silhouetted posture. No identical looping bob on every character.
- **Continuity:** original objects, locations, shapes, symbols and directional movement survive adjacent shots; guide the eye through matched action/matched shape/foreground wipes when beneficial, use clean hard cuts when stronger. Do not overuse dissolves.
- **Depth and simultaneous motion:** 3 separated spatial planes in all major world scenes; both near and far objects can have independent actions. Environmental motion persists under hero motion, but background should not steal contrast from the explanation.
- **Rhythm:** alternate 'dense spectacle' with a 0.3–1.0-second opportunity to comprehend each big revelation. The big reveal itself should not be so fast that nobody notices it.

## C. Attention-energy-desired experience is a concrete shot contract

Each shot should state, in a one-line sheet:
1. What's the viewer's current mental picture?
2. What precise object dominates attention (framing, scale, light, motion)?
3. What movement or reveal CHANGES that picture?
4. What experience results (surprise, 'oh!', curiosity, comedy, wonder)?
5. What visual element/cause carries us into the next shot?
6. Where is the viewer expected to look at the cut? (screen position / action continuity)

If this cannot be answered for a shot, the scene is decorative, not directed. Replace it.

## D. The high-end 'proof frames'

Generate these five ACTUAL beautiful 1920x1080 representative frames (SVG composition/PNG screenshots), cohesive style, not rough diagrams:
1. **0:02:** Our human stands by a meticulously designed kitchen window. An impossible stream of visualized neutrinos passes through the umbrella, walls and the person's body. Big focal event + many small environmental actions.
2. **0:29:** Camera has transformed the kitchen window glint into the Sun's incandescent interior. Layers of fusion elements, golden convection-inspired flows and distinct neutrino streams; detailed luminous art, not yellow concentric circles.
3. **0:56:** Earth's night side, nearly transparent to the visual metaphor: city and mountain silhouettes, atmospheric ring, solar neutrino streams passing through the globe, human remains a tiny readable anchor. Avoid implying every neutrino gets through.
4. **1:34:** Eye-level inside IceCube detector. Dark blue depth, multiple floating optical sensor strings and one staged rare collision causing asymmetric Cherenkov light seen by neighboring sensors.
5. **1:55:** Return to the human in the room, universe reframed as a river flowing through the world; callback to initial umbrella; warm emotional payoff and clear composition.

Require genuinely finished **illustration density** at 1080p. Don't allow the entire film to be a single still plus pan. Ensure actual art layers, articulated characters and camera geometry enable the required movement.

## E. Mandatory motion proof before full production

Build and render a 12-second connected scene from the opening through initial scale transition, not a 12-second slideshow. It must show:
- character visibly reacting with articulated motion,
- symbolic neutrino streaks passing *through* correctly masked foreground / background layers,
- a meaningful camera move with occlusion/parallax,
- clear cause and effect at normal playback speed,
- at least one nontrivial object-to-world transition where shapes visibly transform,
- independent secondary environment movement.

If it fails any property, correct the specific cause (composition, assets, timing or transforms) and rerender only that proof. Limit to two tightly scoped corrections before declaring a blocker; don't silently simplify the creative intention.

## F. Sound

This is a YouTube narrated SCIENCE explainer. An intelligible synchronized original voiceover is part of the storytelling spine. Use a free available/local narration capability when possible, not an unexpected paid API. Add concise original non-copyrighted SFX for impacts, transitions, microscopic clicks and rare detection; modest original/appropriately licensed background audio if available. Music must stay below dialogue; never claim audio complete if absent. If narration quality is a blocker, label the voice as temporary, render the visual test, and report `NOT FINAL AUDIO QUALITY`.
