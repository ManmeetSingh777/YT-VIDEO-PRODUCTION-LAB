## 1. Creative repair: the award is a human moment, not a test prop
V0.1 arranged applause, award, customer, delivery of a private financial sheet and background business like a diagnostic checklist. It felt staged. Keep the contrast, remove fake ceremony. At lunch rush, a longtime regular waiting at the front counter wants a quick photo of Mia with a small local dining plaque that arrived earlier. Mia enjoys the recognition, but simultaneously needs yesterday's closing sheet from her shift lead. She asks for it before it arrives. The lead brings a normal owner-only daily summary (reconciled sales and allocated costs, NOT a POS printout somehow knowing all accounting expenses), presents it toward Mia, shielded from the regular. Award has a self-standing base; she sets it safely on the counter rather than implausibly gripping both a bulky award and opening folded paper. A single page replaces the folded document. The customer isn't a fool: they see a visibly busy business and sincerely assume it is doing well. Humor comes from dramatic irony and Mia's social recovery, not a staged punchline. No applause, no photo flash effect, no unnecessary server crossing or animated crowds for tests. Three principal actors and modest purposeful background life only.

**Viewer promise:** First frame shows packed dining room, a proud owner and signature award. Before 10s, viewer sees yesterday's high sales and actual operating loss AND Mia's reaction. No title card, host, generic explainer or unrelated flashback. A brief close-up of the sheet is motivated by Mia reading it. At 13s she still has to face the customer, not a resolved gag. Next sequence freezes a socially held smile and tells us why this matters. C01 plaque depicts steak, later replayed; no claim of best-selling product (flatbread outsells it by units). Story is X; financial figures M01. Fiction disclosure before explanatory model in B02 (unobtrusive but clear); no claim to be a real rescue.
