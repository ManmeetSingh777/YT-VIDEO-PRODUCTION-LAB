## 1. Mandatory fifteen-field approval page (Bible §20.4)
**Episode:** E01, The Rescue; candidate title *This Restaurant Is Packed. Why Is It Dying?* Fictional Mia, actual economic mechanisms, disclosed model.

1. **Beginning viewer belief:** A full dining room and the restaurant’s most celebrated signature dish mean the owner is doing well.
2. **Human goal / emotional stakes:** Mia has spent three years building a restaurant where customers know her and staff depend on her. She wants to pay the overdue supplier and next wages without sacrificing her signature dish or making her team absorb the cost. She fears the restaurant's identity is inseparable from that dish. Defined fictional cash deadline: day 10 obligations, no miraculous investor. She must decide what to change, not wait to be saved by host.
3. **Main hidden rule in ≤15 simple words:** When capacity is full, earn more from what is scarce, not just each sale. (14 words.) Boundary: customers must actually want the alternative and other resources must support it.
4. **First obvious strategy:** “More people, more money”: try a $1 blanket discount in the host's explicit replay/model of a rush. Mia proposes it because empty seats on earlier weekdays once worried her; host initially agrees. Don't knowingly burn cash on a proven bad trial.
5. **Why it appears to work:** More people accept the offer, orders accumulate, Mia sees the queue and cash register ringing. It resembles the early days when spare capacity made promotion useful.
6. **Why it fails or reaches a limit:** Same completed mix, 160 meals; full scheduled grill zones prevent extra fulfillment. $1 discount lowers contribution by $155.20 after fee effect; model −$275.20 vs −$120. Extra customers cannot be counted as revenue. If demand mix/spare capacity differs, discount may work.
7. **Two/three early clues planted:** C01 award depicts signature steak and regular specifically praises it; C02 in kitchen a flatbread finishes while steak remains on occupied zone; C03 a waiting party asks about a faster option while prep/plating have some available capacity. Only C01 in S001; C02/C03 in subsequent lived-world shots, not background gimmicks. Later tally shows denied diners' preferences, not all demand interchangeable.
8. **Better move(s), costs and boundary:** Compare pricing (M03), protected dinner mix (M04) and safe process redesign (M05). Proposed bounded middle mix: retain lunch 20S/50F, dinner 36S/80F instead of 50S/40F; reserve signature slots, offer genuinely appealing $17 flatbread, truthful estimated wait, no coercion. K=$60/day and $300 one-time cost. +$24/day modeled vs baseline −$120; middle requires 10 voluntary switches and 30 recovered diners; seat and labor schedule conditional. Favorable +$140, adverse −$160. Pricing can outperform; no universal winner. No commitment to final policy without test.
9. **Evidence for each move:** F01–F05 US association surveys (thin margins, costs), F06 seat-hours and duration, F07 activity/time-driven menu costing, F08 waiting behavior, F09–F10 waste, F11 volume counterevidence, F12 food-safety framework; M01–M08 transparent arithmetic, H01–H03 explicit uncertainties. Sources, dates, limits and counterexamples in model document. No historical chain story without relevant evidence.
10. **One counterexample / failed scenario:** Remove 20 dinner steaks but gain only 30 flatbreads; −$160/day (worse than baseline). All-flatbread dinner with 85 buyers yields −$430 (M07). Price retaining same demand earns +$103.10 and beats middle mix. Thus demand, alternative prices, staffing and seating can overturn a per-slot ranking.
11. **Main “I GET IT!” moment:** Two identical 12-minute zones in a split comparison: one steak contributes $14; three flatbreads can use the same time and contribute $30, IF three actual buyers and service capacity exist. Slot-minute rate $1.17 vs $2.50. Host realizes his “best product” calculation missed what the grill could make instead; Mia notices the orders her team couldn't serve. Neither suddenly declares steak bad.
12. **Supporting Value Strikes:** VS01–VS05 in §2; exact recognition → surprise → decode → test → transfer progression. Primary strike VS03; other strikes strengthen rather than interrupt it.
13. **Final emotional character beat:** After a bounded trial, Mia pays defined obligations, retains $808 unrestricted cash (M06 warning zone, not success guarantee). She marks another review rather than celebrates wealth. After closing the cook places her signature steak at a table; she reflexively asks how long it took; “We're closed, Mia.” Smile becomes genuine, unlike S001. She can enjoy a dish without sacrificing a paying order when that capacity isn't scarce. Acknowledge ingredient/labor cost; after-hours meal is not free, use planned staff meal already in allowance or avoid extra cost claims. No guarantee the restaurant survives later months. This is a reprieve and changed decision-making, not a miracle.
14. **One/two analogous experiences:** Freelancer with a full schedule compares contribution after project-linked costs per constrained hour, while accounting for client demand and quality. A small workshop with a saturated machine compares contribution from different jobs per machine hour, accounting for downstream stations. One rapid primary echo (freelancer); second only if it adds clarity. No claim these businesses have identical fixed costs.
15. **What viewer can recognize in a new case tomorrow:** Distinguish revenue/activity from money left; identify actual scarce resource at the relevant time, compare opportunity cost, test real demand and downstream constraints, check cash survival separately. Ask “What else could I do with the same scarce capacity?” and “Who actually wants it?”

## 3. Ending-first causal proof and dramatic repairs
**Three-line plot (Bible §5.3):** Mia wants to keep the restaurant and team she built. More demand and high-contribution dishes cannot cover her commitments when the grill is binding in service windows and cash is short. She and a fallible host discover the scarce-capacity tradeoff; Mia runs a limited, customer-respecting test and secures a conditional reprieve, understanding what to test next.

V0.1 problems repaired: (a) “promote steak” should be a plausible intermediate *hypothesis*, not a second foolish actual campaign; (b) full daily capacity doesn't imply every period is full, so show timed service observations; (c) do not invent a forced price rejection; (d) do not claim 60 more buyers from generic queue; (e) do not equate operating profit and cash; (f) make Mia propose the customer-facing compromise because she knows her people; (g) final meal is not the victory, it is the evidence of a changed relationship with work. No drama that requires unsafe food, unpaid labor, hiding prices or forcing buyers.

## 5. Outcome options for human review
- **Recommended:** middle protected-mix trial, +$24/day, $808 cash on day10. Small but tangible reprieve; most defensible if portrayed as explicitly fictional and a trial. Strongest counterargument: $24 is barely viable, price could achieve more with fewer changes and even price-middle yields $1,240.96 day-10 cash vs $808, the emotional relief might feel underpowered. Do not lock mix until there is a believable fictional price-response comparison; if price wins with comparable experience, change the action rather than force the twist. Make the achievement avoiding an irreversible bad choice and paying the defined obligations, not “saved forever.”
- **Alternative:** hybrid price+mix (M08) +$132.64/day under unchanged middle demand; modeled $1565.12 cash on day10. More breathing room but requires separate price retention evidence, extra story complexity, cannot be declared earned without new test. Price-only can legitimately win if enough buyers stay; ending then changes to better pricing informed by capacity, not simplistic menu rescue.
- **Adverse ending:** if recovered demand fails, Mia stops trial, protects payroll, negotiates or considers closing; no magically granted terms. Strong truth but shifts from rescue to limit; needs a different emotional payoff and user approval.
