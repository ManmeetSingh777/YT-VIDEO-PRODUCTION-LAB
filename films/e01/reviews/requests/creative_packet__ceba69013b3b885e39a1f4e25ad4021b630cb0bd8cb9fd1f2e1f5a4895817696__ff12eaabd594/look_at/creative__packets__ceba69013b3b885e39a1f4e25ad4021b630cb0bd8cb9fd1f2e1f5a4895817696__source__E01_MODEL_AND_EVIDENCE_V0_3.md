### 4.4 Seat-time stress and executable capacity check
48 seats, mixed two/four-tops. Idealized individual admissions 10 min before assigned grill start, exit 55 min after start (65-minute visit incl ordering, food, eating and paying). Lunch arrivals ~11:20–13:20, exits by ~14:20; dinner ~16:50–20:20, exits by ~21:20. At 65min dwell, 48×30/65 ≈22.15 covers/half-hour smooth flow; service cap 20 chosen tighter. Full room can coexist with unused nominal seats (party fit, longer waiting, peak arrivals); baseline schedule is an ideal feasibility example, not measured occupancy. S001 takes place during lunch rush where modeled peak 41 occupied individual seats plus party fragmentation; show tables occupied, a few chairs may be empty, no claim of all 48 seats simultaneously used. Real queue/wait is not proof of 60 extra buyers.

Reproducible test (pure Python, integer minute planning; no simulation of actual demand). It enumerates lane placements, prioritizes lower peak seat occupancy and smoother starts, and checks counts. Bitmask determines steak-first vs flat-first per lane; each item starts after preceding item, small offsets use spare lane time. Deterministic ties resolved by iteration. It does not check same-party synchronized service, stochastic dwell, heat, safety or labor task concurrency. Those are real blockers, not silently assumed solved.

```python
from itertools import product
from collections import Counter
A=(28,28,28,28); B=(28,28,28,24)
L=[A]*2+[B]*2; D=[B]*6+[A]
LB=[(5,13)]*2+[(5,12)]*2
DB=[(7,6)]*6+[(8,4)]
DA=[(4,10)]*6+[(6,10)]
DM=[(5,12)]*6+[(6,8)]
DF=[(4,15)]*6+[(6,10)]
def pack(caps,s,f,st=12):
    for ss in product(range(3),repeat=4):
        if sum(ss)!=s or any(n*st>c for n,c in zip(ss,caps)): continue
        rem=[(c-n*st)//4 for n,c in zip(ss,caps)]
        if sum(rem)<f: continue
        fs=[0]*4
        for _ in range(f):
            j=min((j for j in range(4) if fs[j]<rem[j]),key=lambda j:(ss[j]*st+fs[j]*4,j))
            fs[j]+=1
        yield list(zip(ss,fs))
def peak(starts,dwell=65):
    return max((sum(x-10<=t<x-10+dwell for x in starts) for t in range(-10,max(starts,default=0)+dwell)),default=0)
def schedule(caps,counts,st=12):
    starts=[]; chosen=[]
    for b,(c,(s,f)) in enumerate(zip(caps,counts)):
        assert s+f<=20
        best=None
        for p in pack(c,s,f,st):
            for mask in range(16):
                a=[]
                for j,((ns,nf),cap) in enumerate(zip(p,c)):
                    items=([st]*ns+[4]*nf) if mask&(1<<j) else ([4]*nf+[st]*ns)
                    t=b*30+min(j,cap-sum(items))
                    for dur in items: a.append(t); t+=dur
                allst=starts+a; hist=Counter(x//5 for x in allst)
                score=(peak(allst),max(hist.values()),sum(v*v for v in hist.values()))
                if best is None or score<best[0]: best=(score,a,p,mask)
        if best is None: raise ValueError((c,s,f,st))
        starts+=best[1]; chosen.append((best[2],best[3]))
    assert len(starts)==sum(s+f for s,f in counts)
    return peak(starts),peak(starts,75),max(Counter(x//5 for x in starts).values())
for name,c,counts in [('L',L,LB),('D0',D,DB),('DA',D,DA),('DM',D,DM),('DF',D,DF)]:
    print(name,schedule(c,counts))
```

Executed the code above: at 65-minute visits, lunch 41 peak, dinner baseline 31, adverse 32, middle 41, favorable 46; at 75 minutes the respective peaks are 47, 35, 38, 47, 53. Bursts up to 7 grill starts in 5 min. Favorable has almost no seat slack; at 75 minutes the same schedule exceeds 48 by five. Group seating may fail even with 46 covers. At 19 covers per 30 min, smooth average seat usage ≈41.2 at 65min, 47.5 at 75min, 50.7 at 80min. Do not solve with rushing customers: use pacing, reservation slots, actual fit, reject excess orders, or reduce target. A 4-minute slot time is not a four-minute total meal experience. Additional dining time and off-premises substitution require separate cost models.
